Indian Company Law

Indian company law refers to the legal framework that governs the formation, management, and operation of companies in India. The primary legislation governing companies in India is the Companies Act, 2013, which replaced the Companies Act, 1956. This law regulates various aspects of companies, including incorporation, share capital, directors, meetings, and accounts.

Key Details

The Companies Act, 2013, applies to all types of companies, including private and public companies, limited liability partnerships, and one-person companies. The law requires companies to file various forms and returns with the Registrar of Companies, such as Form INC-1 for name reservation, Form INC-7 for incorporation, and Form AOC-4 for filing annual accounts. The law also prescribes thresholds for various purposes, such as a minimum paid-up capital of Rs. 1 lakh for private companies and Rs. 5 lakhs for public companies.

Who Needs This

Indian company law applies to all companies registered in India, including NGOs that are registered as companies under Section 8 of the Companies Act, 2013. This includes companies limited by shares, companies limited by guarantee, and unlimited companies. NGOs that are registered as trusts or societies under other laws, such as the Indian Trusts Act, 1882, or the Societies Registration Act, 1860, are not directly governed by the Companies Act, 2013. However, they may still need to comply with certain provisions of the law, such as those related to tax exemption and foreign contribution.

Step-by-Step Process

To incorporate a company in India, the following steps need to be taken: 1. Obtain a Director Identification Number (DIN) from the Ministry of Corporate Affairs (MCA) portal. 2. Reserve a name for the company using Form INC-1. 3. Prepare and file the Memorandum and Articles of Association. 4. File Form INC-7 for incorporation. 5. Obtain a certificate of incorporation from the Registrar of Companies.

Important Deadlines / Penalties

Companies are required to file their annual accounts and returns within 30 days of the annual general meeting. Failure to file returns on time can result in penalties, including a fine of up to Rs. 1 lakh and imprisonment of up to 3 years. The law also prescribes deadlines for other filings, such as the filing of Form MGT-14 for special resolutions, which needs to be filed within 30 days of the resolution.
