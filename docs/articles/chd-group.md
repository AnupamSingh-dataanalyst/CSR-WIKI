# CHD Group
The CHD Group is a social venture that aims to improve the quality of life of underprivileged communities in India. Founded in 2010, the group focuses on providing healthcare, education, and livelihood opportunities to marginalized sections of society. With a strong presence in several states across India, the CHD Group has made significant contributions to the development sector.

## Key Details
The CHD Group has a diverse range of initiatives, including healthcare programs, educational institutions, and skill development centers. The group has partnered with various organizations, including government agencies, corporations, and NGOs, to amplify its impact. Some key statistics about the CHD Group include:
* Over 1 million beneficiaries across India
* Presence in 10 states across the country
* Partnerships with over 50 organizations

## Who Needs This
The CHD Group's initiatives are primarily targeted at underprivileged communities, including rural populations, urban slum dwellers, and marginalized groups such as women and children. NGOs and companies working in the development sector, particularly those focused on healthcare, education, and livelihoods, may find the CHD Group's work relevant to their own goals and objectives.

## Step-by-Step Process
For organizations looking to partner with the CHD Group, the process typically involves:
1. Initial outreach and introduction to the group's work and initiatives
2. Discussion of potential partnership opportunities and areas of collaboration
3. Development of a memorandum of understanding (MoU) outlining the terms and scope of the partnership
4. Implementation of joint programs and initiatives

## Important Deadlines / Penalties
As the CHD Group is a social venture, it is subject to various regulations and compliance requirements. Some important deadlines and penalties to note include:
* Annual filing of Form FC-4 with the Ministry of Home Affairs, which is due by December 31st of each year
* Compliance with the Foreign Contribution (Regulation) Act, 2010, which requires NGOs to maintain detailed records of foreign contributions and expenditures
